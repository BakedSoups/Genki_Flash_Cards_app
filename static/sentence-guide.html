Understand the sentence

Read the pattern, notice each particle, then change one word or the tense. The cards keep your original wording; the examples below show other ways to use it.

The essentials: dictionary form, tense, and particles

Find the dictionary form: identify the verb and its group first. For a ru-verb, tabemasu → taberu. For a u-verb, nomimasu → nomu (i-row → u-row). Shimasu → suru and kimasu → kuru are irregular. You cannot reliably turn every -masu into -ru: kaerimasu → kaeru, but kaeru is a u-verb. Learn the dictionary form and group together.

Plain vs polite: taberu / tabemasu mean eat or will eat; context supplies the time. Tabeta / tabemashita mean ate. A daily routine uses nonpast forms. Polite past is -masu → -mashita; polite negative past is -masen deshita.

Plain past: ru-verbs drop ru + ta (taberu → tabeta). U-verbs: u/tsu/ru → tta; mu/bu/nu → nda; ku → ita; gu → ida; su → shita. Exceptions: iku → itta, suru → shita, kuru → kita. Plain negatives end in nai; nai → nakatta makes them past.

Te-form is not past tense: use the same sound changes as plain past, but te/de instead of ta/da. Tabete links “eat and…”; tabeta says “ate.” In a chain such as abite, tabete, iku, change the final iku → itta to put the sequence in the past. Te + imasu can describe an ongoing action or a continuing state; te + imashita puts it in the past.

Adjectives and nouns: i-adjective atsui → atsukatta; negative atsukunai → atsukunakatta. Ii/yoi → yokatta. Nouns and na-adjectives use da → datta, desu → deshita; janai → janakatta. Suki, kirai, and jouzu are na-adjectives. They are not verbs.

Particles: wa marks the topic; ga marks the subject or what is liked / done well; o marks an object; ni marks a destination, existence location, specific time, or recipient; de marks where an action happens or the means used; to means “with,” joins nouns, or quotes a clause; no links nouns or turns a verb phrase into a noun. Their job depends on the sentence.

Swap a whole unit: replace a noun while keeping its particle (basu de → densha de). Replace a verb while choosing the right group and particles (terebi o miru → ongaku o kiku). In verb + no ga patterns, keep the verb in dictionary form for a general activity; change suki desu → suki deshita to say you used to like it.
